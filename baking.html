Baking

I fell in love with baking very recently. Starting off with simple cakes like chocolate or streusel until I tried more difficult stuff later. Now I'm at a point where I want to make literally everything by myself from scratch. This year was life-changing for me when a friend gave me a sourdough starter and I attempted my first sourdough breads. That was my point of no return. Now, I try to use my sourdough as an addition whenever possible (granola, pancakes, sauces). Anyways, here some of my favorite things to bake and their recipe:

Sourdough Bread
Since I make this so much, I had to list this first. All you need is a starter which you can get from someone who already has one or make it yourself. I honestly got mine from my brother and a friend. But once you have your starter, your feed it and wait till it rises so it's ready for the dough. I don't follow a strict recipe since the result varies depending on ingredient quality, temperature and mixing. But for a small loaf I use about 80 g of my ready starter, 250-260 g of water, 400 g of bread flour and 8 g of salt. Mixing/Kneading it all together by hand until it stays in one piece and resists a little bit (about 5 minutes). Then cover it with a damp towel and stretch and fold every 40-60 mins 3-4 times in total. By the end, my dough is very stretchy and has developed a nice gluten network. Then bulk ferment until roughly doubled in size (usually 6-9 hours). Then I shape it and put it in a banneton to cold ferment overnight and bake it in the morning. Scoring is one of my favorite parts, where I can turn my bread into an artwork. I don't have a dutch oven, so I just simulate it by putting a tray filled with water at the bottom for the steam and another tray upside down at the top to trap the steam. Just using a dutch oven might be simpler and give better results but this method has always worked for me quite well. To mix things up I tried my luck with fun add-ins such as caramelized onions with cheddar or strawberry with chocolate. Once you have the basics down of baking normal bread, it's quite fun to just experiment a bit. I know sourdough seems a bit scary and difficult but no first loaf is perfect. It takes some attempts until you get a pretty decent bread. But trust me, once you get that first perfect loaf, you won't want to stop making it. Nothing beats that feeling of taking out a beautiful sourdough out of the oven and cutting into it. So don't let first failures discourage you!

Tiramisu
I made Tiramisu at almost every party/meetup with friends. It's simple, quick, fail-proof and reliable. I use two eggs, 250 g of mascarpone, 100 g of ladyfingers and 30-50 g of sugar (depending on how sweet I want it). Separate yolk and whites in two bowls. Beat the egg whites fluffy with half of the sugar and beat the yolk creamy with the other half. Then add the mascarpone to the yolk and mix it all homogenous. I also like to add some vanilla extract. Then fold it into the egg whites and you got your cream. For the layering dip the ladyfingers 2-3 seconds in coffee and arrange the layers. Ideally, let it sit overnight. When serving, sift cocoa powder on top and enjoy!

Brookies
Brookies - a fusion of Brownies and Cookies. We decided to make these quite spontaneously but they ended up being extremely well received with multiple people requesting the recipe. For the cookie batter, you'll need around 110 g of melted and cooled butter, brown sugar, white sugar, one egg, 1 tbsp vanilla extract, flour, 1/2 tsp baking soda, 1/2 tsp salt, 200 g of chocolate. You mix the butter, sugar, egg and vanilla extract fluffy before combining it with the dry ingredients and chopped chocolate. For the Brownie batter, you'll need 220 g of butter, cocoa powder, 1 tsp vanilla extract, brown sugar, powdered sugar, 200g chocolate, 3 eggs, flour, pinch of salt, 3 tbsp olive oil. Brown the butter in a saucepan until milk solids turn golden brown and smell nutty. Put it in heatproof bowl and mix it with cocoa powder, oil, vanilla and 3/4 of the chopped chocolate (the chocolate should melt). In another bowl, whisk eggs and sugar until the sugar is dissolved (5 minutes, this ensures a crackly top). Add powdered sugar and mix again until all combined. Finally, add the cooled butter mixture and fold in the remaining chocolate pieces. Now layer it however you like and bake it for 40 minutes at 175 °C. When it's done, it will be a bit jiggly in the middle. Let it cool completely before serving.
